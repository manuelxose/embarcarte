It is defined as a complete article: panel, compute module, storage, enclosure, specified mounting subject to the installation assessment, power conversion and protection, marine connectors and harness, a dedicated operating environment and the base software that presents navigation, instruments, received AIS traffic, licensed data layers and the approved status surfaces.

## 8. Features and capabilities
**A physical product with a dedicated environment.** The display is defined with a panel, a compute module, storage, an enclosure, a specified mount family subject to the installation assessment, power protection and marine connectors. The operating environment boots into the instrument interface, not into a general-purpose desktop. No component is selected yet and no measurement is published.

**Light modes.** A daylight mode for the helm, a sunlight mode for direct light and a low-luminance night mode that reduces glare and disables pulsing. All three keep the alarm hierarchy intact. No brightness, contrast or sunlight-legibility figure is claimed: none has been measured.

**Configurable helm dashboards.** The owner composes helm views from approved widgets with factory presets, a preview and a documented limit.

**Navigation and instruments.** Presentation of position, course, route and waypoints as a navigational aid, and presentation of wind, depth, battery, tanks and utility states only from compatible, verified sources, with unit, source and age.

**Received AIS traffic.** Received targets are presented as traffic information with their source and age and with the receive-only notice. The display does not receive AIS by itself: it needs a compatible, installed receiver. It is not a collision-avoidance system.

**Licensed charts and layers.** Charts are shown only within the coverage and terms of the licence held; a weather layer only with a MAREA WEATHER entitlement and provider rights for that layer and area. If the licence does not exist, the layer is not shown: its real state is displayed.

**Data with provenance.** Every value shows its source and age where the source allows it; stale, unavailable or not-evaluated states are marked explicitly.

**Diagnostics and backup.** A source, power and freshness check that produces a readable record for the installer and support; export and restore of settings and profiles in a documented format.

**Updates and recovery.** A signed update with a return to the previous version if the update fails or is interrupted, preserving configuration. This is a design requirement; it is not yet implemented or tested.

## 9. Realistic usage scenarios
**A weekend trip with existing instruments.** The owner powers on the display at the helm, selects the vessel profile and sees position and the compatible instruments the installer configured. If a depth sensor or an instrument stops responding, that value is marked stale with its age and the rest remains available. Watchkeeping and decisions remain with the skipper.

**A coastal passage with received traffic.** With a compatible AIS receiver installed, received targets appear as traffic information with their reception time and the notice that not every vessel transmits AIS. If the receiver loses coverage, targets are marked stale and the traffic advisory is suspended instead of being computed from old data.

**A night at the helm.** The owner switches to night mode: luminance drops, pulsing disappears and the alarm hierarchy remains distinguishable without relying on colour. The display does not become a source of glare.

**A power failure in the berth.** The display shuts down or enters a defined safe state; configuration is not lost. When power is restored it boots into a known state and shows the software version and the sources it finds. This is the intended behaviour; it has not been measured.

## 13. Compatibility
MAREA DISPLAY compatibility is assessed per device, per firmware version and per data direction, and is published in a compatibility matrix. There is no universal compatibility: no device is assumed compatible because of its brand, category or connector.

- **Data sources:** every instrument, gateway or receiver is checked separately, with its model and firmware version.
- **NMEA 2000:** its status is unresolved in the programme; no NMEA 2000 certification and no operational compatibility with that standard is claimed.
- **Signal K:** it is not presented as a general compatibility guarantee.
- **Charts and data:** compatibility includes the coverage and terms of the licence, not only the file format.
- **MAREA NAV:** the display may present navigation where the interface exists; MAREA NAV is a separate software product and is not included.
- **MAREA PILOT:** read-only status and alarms at most, in a future authorised interface; never steering, pilot engagement or disengagement, heading adjustment or remote control.
- **No control path:** no public steering control path is offered or implied from the display.

Before any intended purchase, the installation and compatibility review of your vessel is the step that determines which configuration is valid and what is out of scope.

## 14. How it works
1. **You review the helm** and confirm with an installer the space, mounting surface, power and available data sources.
2. **The display is installed** with the specified mount family, subject to the installation assessment, and the documented power protection.
3. **Only documented sources are connected** and the installation check is run.
4. **You start the display:** a short boot shows the software version, the vessel profile and the sources found.
5. **You create or select the vessel profile** and choose the language and light mode.
6. **You compose your dashboard** from the presets and approved widgets.
7. **You use the display** with the source and age of every value visible, and you know what to do if a source is missing or stale.
8. **You export your configuration** whenever you want, to keep it or restore it on a replacement unit.
