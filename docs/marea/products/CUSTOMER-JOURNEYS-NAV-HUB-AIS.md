## 1. Purpose, status and reading conventions

This document models the **finished commercial journey** of the three batch-1 products: what the customer does, what they see, what they must know, where they misunderstand the offer, what the company must have operational before that step can be honest, and which later milestone proves the step works. It is a planning artefact for four audiences:

- the **UX writer and content owner** (D8 copy, page structure, CTA and disclaimer placement);
- the **front-end and commerce developer** (which states, entities and service calls each step implies);
- the **operations planner** (what support, fulfilment, installation and records must exist before a step is exposed);
- the **milestone owners** in `WEBSITE-AND-ROADMAP.md` §6 (MC-15, MC-18/20, MC-23, MC-24, MC-26, MC-27, MC-28, MC-14 and the MC-13 verification gates).

Nothing here is implemented. The three products are `DEFINED` and `NOT_FOR_SALE`; no capability in this document is verified, sellable or publishable, and no step is claimed to be live. A journey that is fully specified is still a target.

### 1.1 Status vocabulary used for journeys

| Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `SPECIFIED_ONLY` | The step is defined here; no page, service, entitlement or process exists to execute it |
| `OPEN` | The intended behaviour depends on an unresolved internal decision or measurement |
| `BLOCKED` | An external condition (licence, certificate, contract, hardware, brand clearance) prevents the step from being offered or claimed |
| `PARTIAL_EVIDENCE` | A related capability exists in the internal prototype foundation but is untested and not product-integrated |
| `NOT_APPLICABLE` | The stage does not apply to that product, with the reason stated |

A step may carry more than one label across its columns; the acceptance column names the gate that resolves it.

### 1.2 CTA and language rules (binding for every touchpoint)

- All three products are at lifecycle `DEFINED`. The only allowed CTAs are the claims-policy §5 equivalents of **"See the specification"** / «Ver la especificación», **"Request a compatibility review"** / «Solicitar estudio de compatibilidad», and an explicitly authorised interest list where one exists. No purchase, cart, reservation, stock or delivery CTA is permitted at any stage of this document.
- The navigation disclaimer is required on every surface showing position, charts, traffic, depth or weather; the AIS receive-only disclaimer is required on every AIS surface; the weather disclaimer wherever forecasts appear (`COMMERCIAL-BRAND-CLAIMS-POLICY.md` §9, carried verbatim in D1–D3). The copy is owned by D8 and is not duplicated here.
- Spanish customer copy uses **tú**; English uses neutral professional "you". Product, edition and bundle names are invariant across languages.
- No certification, approval, ECDIS status, NMEA 2000 certification or compatibility, guaranteed reception, collision-avoidance, safety guarantee, availability, stock, lead time, installer duration, support SLA, warranty length, testimonial or customer count may appear in any journey touchpoint. AIS is always described as receive-only.

### 1.3 Price rule for journey stages

No approved price exists. The programme price hypotheses are recorded **only as internal planning figures**: MAREA NAV Essential EUR 99/year and Pro EUR 149/year; MAREA HUB Standard EUR 349 and Pro EUR 499; MAREA AIS Receiver EUR 249 and Connected EUR 399 — every one `HYPOTHESIS_UNAPPROVED`, VAT-inclusive retail hypothesis where stated. **No journey touchpoint may display any of these numbers until a costed, approved price list exists** (`D6`, `X-05`); the customer-facing state is a specification view, a compatibility review request or an authorised interest list. Installation and licensed data are always quoted or licensed separately and are never described as included.

### 1.4 Proposed routes are not implemented

Route paths named below are proposed public addresses from `WEBSITE-AND-ROADMAP.md` §2 (`/marea/nav`, `/en/marea/nav`, `/marea/hub`, `/marea/ais`, `/marea/configurador`, `/soporte/marea/:slug`, `/instaladores`, `/profesionales`). None is live, none has passed route-collision or canonical/hreflang validation, and no journey below may be built before that validation and the brand/readiness gates.

## 5. Cross-product journeys

Eight journeys cover the realistic ways the three products meet. Each is the intended final experience: the data flow, the customer result, the entitlement boundary and the blocked steps. All are `SPECIFIED_ONLY`; none is verified, and no journey may be used to imply that any combination is currently purchasable. A journey that is blocked in its middle stages is still useful as a specification target: it fixes what the customer must be told before each purchase decision, which is where most misunderstanding occurs.

### XJ-1 — MAREA NAV standalone

- **Data flow.** Device GNSS → NAV; licensed chart source → NAV; customer's own routes and logbook stored in the account.
- **Customer result.** Plan, navigate and record on one device with device positioning and an entitlement-limited chart view; no HUB, AIS or instrument required.
- **Entitlement boundary.** NAV Essential is sufficient for this journey. Charts, tides, currents and forecasts are licensed data entitlements, not included. No AIS hardware, no HUB, no installation.
- **Step list.** 1 Discover the product page; 2 read the capability layers and exclusions; 3 run the device compatibility check; 4 choose Essential against the declared use; 5 understand the commercial model without an unapproved price; 6 request a specification view or compatibility review; 7 (after readiness) receive account and entitlement; 8 download, install and activate with a licensed chart entitlement; 9 plan and save a first route; 10 navigate and log passages; 11 receive updates; 12 contact support; 13 upgrade if needs change; 14 renew, cancel and export at term end; 15 export and delete at end of life.
- **Blocked today.** Chart rights (`D-01`), offline/region storage rights (`D-03`), account/entitlement/billing build (`E-19`, `E-20`, MC-11A), approved price and sale (`X-05`, `X-01`). Steps 6–9 cannot be executed.
- **Commercial significance.** This is the entry point and the cheapest proof that MAREA can sell software without inventory; it also produces the customer's first exportable record, which is the retention base.

### XJ-2 — MAREA NAV + MAREA HUB

- **Data flow.** Supported vessel sources → HUB normalisation → documented local interface → NAV Pro instrument overlays; NAV keeps working without the HUB.
- **Customer result.** The boat's wind, depth, speed, heading and utility context appear inside the navigation workspace, each with source, timestamp and stale state.
- **Entitlement boundary.** Two separate purchases: NAV subscription (Pro recommended for live instrument overlays) and the HUB appliance with professional installation outside the documented self-install set. The HUB does not include a NAV licence; NAV does not include a HUB. Instrument compatibility is per the published matrix, never assumed.
- **Step list.** 1 Discover NAV and HUB; 2 establish that NAV works without HUB and HUB does not include NAV; 3 assess the vessel equipment and HUB compatibility; 4 choose NAV Pro if live overlays are needed; 5 understand the two price bases and separate installation; 6 request compatibility review/quote; 7 (after readiness) purchase both through approved SKUs; 8 install and commission HUB; 9 activate NAV and link the account; 10 confirm instrument sources appear with source and freshness; 11 use and update both; 12 support through the correct owner; 13 add or upgrade later; 14 plan renewal and export.
- **Blocked today.** HUB hardware and frozen protocol matrix (`P-02`, `H-05`, `H-07`); NAV/HUB interface contract (`D5`); installation service and quotes (`X-05`, `X-06`); edition upgrade mechanics (`D4` §8). Steps 3–9 blocked.
- **Commercial significance.** This is the first "use the boat you already own" journey: it turns the HUB from a component into a reason to subscribe to NAV Pro, and it is the most likely source of installer-led revenue once hardware exists.

### XJ-3 — MAREA NAV + MAREA AIS

- **Data flow.** AIS VHF antenna (or splitter) → receive-only receiver → documented local output → NAV AIS input → target list/overlay; NAV uses its own position, COG and SOG for any proximity computation.
- **Customer result.** One surface combines navigation and receive-only traffic awareness, with target age and source visible; the customer can tell stale from live.
- **Entitlement boundary.** NAV subscription plus AIS hardware; the AIS antenna/splitter, cabling and installation are separate; NAV licence is not included with AIS. Basic AIS target display is in both NAV editions; CPA/TCPA and alert rules are Pro per D4. D5 `A-05` assigns CPA/TCPA computation to the consuming application (MAREA NAV Pro) and `C-05` assigns the own-vessel position/COG/SOG supply to the AIS/NAV/HUB data path, so the rendering and data-supply boundary is fixed; the missing evidence is test evidence, not an ownership decision.
- **Step list.** 1 Discover NAV and AIS; 2 read the receive-only limitation and the separate purchases; 3 assess display/consumer compatibility and antenna/splitter route; 4 choose NAV edition and AIS edition; 5 understand the two price bases and installation; 6 request compatibility review/quote; 7 (after readiness) install antenna/splitter and receiver; 8 activate NAV and confirm AIS input; 9 see target list/overlay with age and source; 10 check CPA/TCPA or alerts only where valid and supported; 11 update and support both; 12 upgrade only within the decided path; 13 renew NAV, export records and cancel if desired.
- **Blocked today.** Receiver hardware and RF conformity (`H-08`, `P-03`), AIS-derived data retention (`D-09`), tested NAV integration and matrix (`D5`, `P-10`). Steps 3–11 blocked.
- **Commercial significance.** This is the clearest cross-sell in the batch and the one where over-promising is most dangerous: receive-only and incomplete traffic must be explained before the sale, not after.

### XJ-4 — MAREA HUB + MAREA AIS

- **Data flow.** Antenna/splitter → receiver → HUB local data layer → consumers on the vessel network. Per D4, managed HUB integration is a MAREA AIS Connected capability; Receiver provides a direct single documented output.
- **Customer result.** Mixed equipment and traffic data reach one local data layer; AIS is available to more than one compatible consumer where the Connected design evidences it.
- **Entitlement boundary.** HUB and AIS are separate hardware purchases; HUB does not include AIS and AIS does not include HUB. HUB integration is `Connected` only; `Receiver` serves one compatible consumer. No NAV licence implied. Installation separate.
- **Step list.** 1 Discover HUB and AIS; 2 establish the separate-purchase and edition boundaries; 3 assess HUB compatibility and receiver/antenna plan together; 4 choose HUB edition and AIS edition (Connected for managed HUB integration); 5 understand separate hardware and installation costs; 6 request assessment/quote; 7 (after readiness) install HUB and receiver; 8 configure the receiver as a HUB source; 9 confirm traffic and other vessel data in the local dashboard; 10 diagnose with HUB diagnostics; 11 update and support both; 12 transfer or retire the equipment.
- **Blocked today.** HUB hardware/protocol matrix (`P-02`, `H-05`), receiver hardware (`H-08`), AIS data rights (`D-09`), shared target-state vocabulary and transport (`D5`). Steps 3–11 blocked.
- **Commercial significance.** This journey sells integrated electronics rather than two boxes: it is the strongest justification for the Connected edition, provided the hardware evidences multi-client distribution and diagnostics.

### XJ-5 — MAREA NAV + MAREA HUB + MAREA AIS

- **Data flow.** Supported vessel sources → HUB → local interface → NAV (instruments and receive-only AIS overlay); NAV's own position/COG/SOG feeds any proximity computation; all under separate licences.
- **Customer result.** A coordinated onboard data picture in one navigation workspace: plan, navigate, log, see instruments and receive-only traffic.
- **Entitlement boundary.** Three separate purchases plus installation and licensed chart data. NAV Pro is the edition that consumes live instruments and CPA/TCPA/alerts; basic AIS display remains in both NAV editions. No bundle price or content is decided (`MC-08`); MAREA CRUISE is ecosystem context only. No double billing of any shared entitlement.
- **Step list.** 1 Discover the three products and what each does not include; 2 establish that no product includes another and no bundle is purchasable; 3 assess the whole vessel configuration; 4 choose editions per product; 5 understand three price bases plus installation and licensed data; 6 request a coordinated assessment and quote; 7 (after readiness) install HUB, receiver and antenna/splitter; 8 purchase NAV and activate the account; 9 confirm instrument data and AIS traffic in NAV; 10 use, update and support; 11 upgrade within decided paths; 12 renew NAV, export own data; 13 transfer or retire hardware.
- **Blocked today.** Union of XJ-2 and XJ-3 blockers, plus D5 end-to-end matrix and MC-08 bundle governance. Steps 3–14 blocked.
- **Commercial significance.** This is the coordinated offer the MAREA ecosystem exists to make possible, but it must be sold as three assessed purchases, not as a turnkey system: the customer needs to see which product delivers which outcome and what each does not include.

### XJ-6 — MAREA NAV + MAREA WEATHER entitlement sharing

- **Data flow.** MAREA WEATHER entitlement (a separate service and licence) → account entitlement mapping → NAV forecast/tide/current overlays where licensed.
- **Customer result.** One account shows licensed forecast layers with provider, issue time and validity window, without paying twice for the identical entitlement.
- **Entitlement boundary.** WEATHER is a separate licensed service; no weather licence for every provider exists; a NAV subscription does not automatically include a WEATHER entitlement. **Where a NAV Pro entitlement includes a WEATHER entitlement, the same entitlement must not be billed twice** — this is an intended model, not implemented. Whether WEATHER is bundled into NAV Pro or always separately entitled remains `OPEN` (`D4` E-07). Until the mapping exists and is tested, no customer-facing "no double billing" promise may be made (`COMMERCIAL-BRAND-CLAIMS-POLICY.md` §6). Cancelling one entitlement must show exactly which data disappears.
- **Step list.** 1 Customer discovers WEATHER as a separate service while reading NAV; 2 confirms which surfaces need forecasts and which region; 3 checks whether a WEATHER entitlement already exists on the account; 4 understands that no weather licence covers every provider and that NAV does not include one; 5 (after implementation) links the shared entitlement to the NAV account; 6 sees forecasts with provider, issue time and validity window; 7 cancels or renews each entitlement with a clear statement of what disappears; 8 keeps own records exportable.
- **Blocked today.** Weather provider terms (`D-04`, `D-05`, `D-07`, `D-10`), entitlement mapping implementation (`E-19`, MC-11A), WEATHER product decisions (MC-08). Steps 5–15 blocked.
- **Commercial significance.** Data licences are the largest recurring cost in the NAV business: this journey is how the company avoids charging the same customer twice and how it keeps the licensed-data boundary visible in the offer.

### XJ-7 — Installer-led retrofit (professional)

- **Data flow.** Vessel survey and equipment inventory → one D5 §9.2 configuration outcome → proposal and materials → installation and configuration → function checks and commissioning record → handover and training → support and later upgrades across NAV, HUB and AIS as purchased.
- **Customer result.** A documented installation with a named responsibility boundary, an installation record and a handover that explains what works, what does not and what is licensed.
- **Entitlement boundary.** Installation is a separately quoted service, never bundled into a product price; installer workmanship and first-line support are the installer's, product support and matrix authority are EMBARCARTE's. No technician certification scheme, measured duration or installation price exists; the installer channel terms are `OPEN`.
- **Step list.** 1 Customer contacts an installer or requests one through the site; 2 pre-sale questionnaire and vessel survey; 3 equipment inventory with models and firmware where obtainable; 4 one D5 §9.2 configuration outcome against the matrix; 5 written proposal with scope, materials and separate labour; 6 materials sourced (adapters, cabling, antenna/splitter as applicable); 7 installation on the vessel; 8 configuration and function checks; 9 commissioning record; 10 customer handover and training; 11 support boundary agreed between installer and EMBARCARTE; 12 later upgrades assessed separately.
- **Blocked today.** Installer network, agreements, rate card and measured installations (`X-05`, `X-06`, MC-14A); HUB/AIS hardware (`H-05`, `H-07`, `H-08`); compatibility matrix (`D5`); warranty/support terms and seller identity (`X-02`).
- **Commercial significance.** The installer is the trust channel for the entire batch: a documented installation is what converts an uncertain owner, and the installer's first-line support is the only scalable service model while no measured installation data exists.

### XJ-8 — Marina or maintenance company, multi-vessel

- **Data flow.** Each vessel assessed and configured individually; installation records and diagnostics comparable across the fleet; one support relationship; volume or trade terms quoted, never taken from a hypothesis price.
- **Customer result.** A consistent data layer and support path across a small fleet, with per-vessel documentation.
- **Entitlement boundary.** This is a B2B quotation and contract path. MAREA FLEET is a separate SaaS product and is **not implied** by buying HUB, NAV or AIS; no fleet licence, telemetry service or aggregation of AIS-derived data is included. NAV subscriptions, chart/weather licences and installation remain per vessel unless a written agreement says otherwise.
- **Step list.** 1 Marina identifies a need across several vessels; 2 requests a B2B conversation and quotes; 3 each vessel is surveyed and assessed individually; 4 a per-vessel configuration list is produced; 5 one written proposal covers products, installation and support boundaries; 6 orders are placed per vessel under agreed trade terms; 7 installations are scheduled and recorded; 8 handover per vessel; 9 a single support relationship is defined; 10 later expansion is quoted, not assumed.
- **Blocked today.** B2B discovery and terms (`X-06`, `X-05`), multi-tenant/service architecture (`E-17`/`E-18`, not part of this batch), AIS-derived redistribution (`D-09`), installer channel (MC-14A).
- **Commercial significance.** This is the highest-value channel in the region and the easiest place to overreach: each vessel needs its own assessment, and MAREA FLEET must never be implied as included.

### 10.2 Reconciliation items with D1–D4 and D6

1. **CPA/TCPA and traffic-alert layer — resolved and recorded.** D5 `A-05` assigns CPA/TCPA computation to the consuming application (MAREA NAV Pro), and D5 `C-05` assigns the own-vessel position/COG/SOG (and heading where required) supply to the AIS/NAV/HUB data path. D4 `N12`/`N13` place CPA/TCPA and alert rules in NAV Pro; D3 §11 records the same resolution. D7 therefore presents CPA/TCPA and alerts as a navigation-application layer fed by valid AIS targets and own-vessel data, with missing inputs shown as unavailable. What remains is test evidence (`calculateCpa` unit test, integration test; `D-09` retention limits), not an ownership decision.
2. **HUB integration and multi-client distribution are `Connected` capabilities.** D4 (`A05`, `A06`) assigns multi-client local distribution and managed HUB integration to MAREA AIS Connected only; D3 Journey C describes a HUB+AIS path without restating the edition restriction. **D7 follows D4**; D3's journey text should be read as describing the Connected configuration.
3. **MAREA AIS Connected value is conditional.** D3 §3 concludes Connected is not yet justified until the frozen hardware proves multi-client and diagnostics; D4 presents Connected as a real customer difference. **D7 treats the D4 edition structure as authoritative and the D3 verdict as a release condition**: Connected may be described only after the hardware evidence exists.
4. **HUB installation record depth.** D2 requires an installation record as a general HUB capability (F04, CD-17, §7), while D4 gates the documented commissioning record to Pro (`H12`, basic pass/fail in Standard). **D7 follows D4** for the edition split and flags that D2 §7 should state the Standard variant explicitly before the editions are published.
5. **NAV transfer/resale.** No dossier defines whether a NAV subscription can transfer on vessel sale; D6 defines an annual renewable entitlement but not transfer. `D7` marks the stage `OPEN` and forbids any transfer claim until terms exist.
6. **No-double-billing statement.** D1, D4 and D6 describe entitlement sharing as intended and explicitly say it must not be claimed until implemented (`COMMERCIAL-BRAND-CLAIMS-POLICY.md` §6). D7 treats every journey mention as internal intent; customer copy may say nothing about sharing until the mapping and test exist.
