## 1. Why MC-04 needs this dossier at all

MC-04 prototypes three strategic territories and must shortlist **two** of them for a professional
originality/prior-art check before any internal adoption (`MC-04-VISUAL-IDENTITY-BRIEF.md` §3.8 step 5,
§12.7 step 3). Two independent legal facts are already recorded and remain unchanged:

1. **The word is weak on its own.** `MAREA` is an ordinary descriptive/suggestive Spanish, Portuguese,
   Galician and Italian word for *tide*; inherent distinctiveness in the marine field is weak, so any
   potential distinctiveness would have to live in the figurative construction and the family system
   (`MC-04-VISUAL-IDENTITY-BRIEF.md` §3.6; `TRADEMARK-CLEARANCE-RESEARCH.md` §8).
2. **A materially relevant earlier right exists and is live.** A verified European Union trade mark
   whose only verbal element is `MAREA`, in Nice class 9, covering mobile apps and mobile-device
   software, is registered to BRANDS 2020, S.L. (Barcelona). The MC-03 screening classed this as
   **MATERIAL CONFLICT INDICATED — SPECIALIST REVIEW REQUIRED**
   (`TRADEMARK-CLEARANCE-RESEARCH.md` §2, §5.1). That is not an infringement finding; it is a reason
   that the decision cannot be taken inside the programme.

Point 2 has a direct consequence for MC-04 that is often missed in design briefs: the risk is **not
only** the word. The earlier right is a **figurative** mark with a device element recorded in the
register (`M MAREA`, EUTM 018135609; §6.2 below). A figurative construction that generates a similar
device reading — or that is positioned next to the identical word in the same lockup — changes the
comparison. That is precisely the kind of question a professional search answers and an agent must not.

### 5.1 Territory A — "Isobath" (literal-marine: the tide line) — **DROPPED at the MC-04 shortlist (§9)**

> **Status note.** The Lead dropped this territory on the brief §3.3 kill criterion (generic contour/wave
> reading; the prototype reads as signal/Wi-Fi arcs at small size) and its lowest weighted hypothesis
> score (3.35 vs 3.95 / 3.95, §3.2). The geometry below is retained as the record of what was dropped and
> why; it is **not** part of the counsel package.

**Strategic idea.** Drawn from the cartographer's isobath / tide line: a small stack of open contour
lines whose last line doubles as a measured baseline. Reads "this is charted water", "depth is
information", "the tide is a measured phenomenon, not a mood". The most directly Spanish reading of
*marea* and the most serious of the three.

**Geometry (construction hypothesis).**

| Element | Specification |
| --- | --- |
| Grid | 24×24 units, 2-unit stroke |
| Primary structure | 4 nested **open** contours at 4-unit intervals, opening toward the right at a consistent angle; intervals deliberately **unequal (3 / 4 / 5 units)** so the figure cannot reduce to "three generic waves" |
| Anchor | innermost contour ends in a short horizontal datum that optically aligns with the wordmark baseline |
| Reduced variant | 16 px: 3 contours, 2 px strokes on the 16 grid, datum retained |
| Lockup relation | symbol left of `MAREA`; symbol cap-height = wordmark cap-height; 0.75× cap-height gap |
| Optical corrections | contour ends cut perpendicular to the local tangent; no accidental tangencies; open ends must not look like an unfinished sketch |
| Formal family | open line / contour; **no closed shape, no circle, no arc, no letter form** |

**Provenance.** Authored from the rule table above; single ink; no font or icon source; no input image;
no tracing. Construction equations must be recorded in the SVG source comments and in the identity
system deliverable (IdentityVector), not only in prose.

**Design-space crowding already known before any search.** Contour/ring/tide constructions are among
the most used devices in marine, environmental and outdoor branding; the brief itself calls this the
most crowded marine motif in its metaphor evaluation (§3.7). Preliminary public-source observations are
in §7.5 and are unfavourable to the idea of treating this construction as inherently distinctive.

**Apparent similarities vs differences — preliminary, non-adjudicative (§7.5).**

| Apparent similarity | Apparent difference | Residual risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open curved lines / nested contours / concentric rings are a well-established motif in marine and environmental identity work | The unequal 3/4/5 interval system and the terminal datum are our construction rules, not a generic three-wave shape | High crowding means a similarity search must be run even though no specific earlier right is currently recorded against it |
| "Tide"/"Marea" identities in design practice frequently pair the word with a wave-like form | Our figure is an isobath stack (open contours), not a wave silhouette | The composite MAREA + wave-like figure is the exact combination a search agent must examine |
| The word itself carries the tide meaning in the primary market | — | Descriptiveness pressure: a literal tide drawing strengthens the descriptive reading (§3.6 consequence 2) |

### 5.2 Territory B — "Bearing index" (literal-marine: the navigation instrument) — **SHORTLISTED (§9)**

**Strategic idea.** An instrument fragment, not a compass rose: a heading index — one bearing line
crossing a calibrated arc with a single position datum. Reads "heading", "bearing", "measured
direction", "the instrument you steer by". The territory that most directly carries navigation
credibility for NAV, DISPLAY and PILOT.

**Geometry (construction hypothesis).**

| Element | Specification |
| --- | --- |
| Grid | 32×32 units; 2.5-unit stroke for the arc, 2-unit for the index |
| Primary structure | a **270° calibrated arc**, open at the bottom-right; one bold radial bearing index at 0°; one small square datum at the centre; **two asymmetric tick marks** (deliberately not a full rose) |
| Reduced variant | 16 px: arc reduced to 1.5 px, only index + datum + one tick retained |
| Lockup relation | mark left of `MAREA`; mark cap-height = wordmark cap-height; the arc's optical centre aligns to the wordmark's mid-cap height |
| Optical corrections | arc ends cut at a consistent angle; bearing index visually heavier than ticks; no accidental "G" or "C" reading |
| Formal family | open arc + radial segment + central square datum; **circular-emblem risk** must be managed |

**Provenance.** Authored from the rule table; single ink; no third-party gauge or compass artwork was
used; no official chart symbology; no font-derived glyph. The "no accidental G or C reading" rule is an
authorship constraint aimed at avoiding letter-form collision, and it must be demonstrated in the
reduced-variant proof, not asserted.

**Design-space crowding already known.** Compass/rose/bearing devices are the most saturated marine
motif (§3.4 risks; §3.7 metaphor table). Two additional risks specific to rights: (i) circular emblems
with an index can read as certification seals — a claims problem and a similarity problem; (ii) a
navigation-authority implication on a navigation aid the programme cannot certify.

**Apparent similarities vs differences — preliminary, non-adjudicative (§7.5).**

| Apparent similarity | Apparent difference | Residual risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Circular instrument motifs with a pointer are the standard visual grammar of the navigation category | A 270° open arc with an asymmetric two-tick vocabulary and a square datum is not the standard compass rose | High: the category grammar itself is old and shared; similarity analysis will focus on overall impression, not on the idea of an arc |
| The earlier right's device element is described as an **oval "M"** (MC-03 record; §6.2) | Territory B is an arc, not an oval enclosing a letter; the datum is a square, not a letter | **Material for the composite**: the same lockup contains the identical word `MAREA`; a professional must compare the whole composite, not our symbol in isolation |
| Gauge/tick vocabulary is used across instrument brands | The mark is deliberately reduced to an instrument *fragment* | The reduction does not by itself create distance from any earlier right |

### 5.3 Territory C — "Signal trunk" (non-literal engineering/system motif) — **SHORTLISTED; `PREFERRED_PROVISIONAL` only (§9)**

> **Decision caveat (recorded, not resolved).** C's "implied `M`" is the closest device-description
> overlap with the earlier figurative mark and is escalated to counsel — see §9.5. If the frozen
> silhouette no longer forms an `M`, that change is recorded honestly and may **reduce** (not eliminate)
> that particular concern; no conclusion of any kind is drawn.

**Strategic idea.** Drawn from the data backbone and the terminal block: a bus rail with three unequal
terminal keys, the middle key raised — the physical grammar of marine installation (connectors, glands,
labelled cores) abstracted to three strokes. Reads "integrated systems", "installed properly", "one
vessel, many systems". Contains no wave, no compass and no boat.

**Geometry (construction hypothesis).**

| Element | Specification |
| --- | --- |
| Grid | 24×24 units, 2-unit stroke |
| Primary structure | one horizontal **trunk line**; three **vertical terminal keys** at unequal spacing (left, centre **raised**, right); terminal caps **squared**, not round; centre key extended to form the spine of an **implied `M`** beside the wordmark |
| Reduced variant | 16 px: two keys + rail at 2 px stroke; the centre key remains the raised one |
| Lockup relation | symbol left of `MAREA`; rail sits on the wordmark baseline; raised key aligns to cap-height |
| Optical corrections | unequal spacing intentional (physical terminals are not evenly spaced on a block); caps must not merge at small size; must not accidentally read as `≡`, `#` or a menu icon |
| Formal family | orthogonal strokes; **letter-form risk (M) by design** |

**Provenance.** Authored from the rule table; single ink; no third-party connector or terminal-block
brand artwork used; the "physical terminals are not evenly spaced" rationale is recorded to prevent the
construction collapsing into a generic three-bar icon later.

**The critical rights observation for this candidate.** Territory C deliberately extends the centre key
"to form the spine of an implied `M`" (§3.5, brief). The recorded earlier right is a **figurative mark
whose device element is an oval "M" above the word `MAREA`** (§6.2, MC-03 record). An implied `M`
standing next to the word `MAREA` is therefore the candidate whose device element is closest, by
description, to the earlier device — and this is exactly the comparison no agent is qualified to
settle. This is a **material risk flag for counsel**, not a finding.

**Apparent similarities vs differences — preliminary, non-adjudicative (§7.5).**

| Apparent similarity | Apparent difference | Residual risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Three-stroke / rail-and-keys constructions are common in technology identity work (see §7.5) | Unequal spacing, squared caps and a raised centre key are specific construction rules, not a generic bar chart | Medium-to-high; must be searched, not assumed |
| The implied `M` element echoes, by description, the letter element of the earlier figurative mark | Our `M` would be an abstract orthogonal structure, not an oval enclosing a letterform | **Material** — the composite carries the identical word; counsel must assess the whole |
| Reads as integrated systems/data backbone | Contains no marine literal cue: marine meaning is carried by the wordmark and family names | Weak marine semantics is a strategic risk, not a rights risk |

   records this "suffix discipline".
2. **A symbol does not neutralise the word.** Because the earlier mark is figurative, our device may
   reduce *or increase* similarity depending on what it looks like next to the identical word. That

3. **Territory C's implied `M` is the construction with the most direct device-description overlap** and

4. **Territory A's literal tide reading** raises a descriptiveness concern (§3.6) that is a rights

5. **No territory is "safer" because it is abstract.** An abstract mark that borrows a category's
   generic grammar (arcs, bars, contours) is not thereby distinctive.
