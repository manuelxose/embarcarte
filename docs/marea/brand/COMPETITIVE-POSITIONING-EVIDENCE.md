- Fetched official manufacturer pages directly where possible (Garmin, Raymarine, B&G, Simrad, Digital Yacht, Orca, Navionics/Garmin, Yacht Devices, Victron). Read only returned page text. Search-engine queries were used only to locate URLs and to capture evidence where a page was unreadable.

- Checked my observations against `MARKET-BENCHMARKS.md` — no contradictions found; this pack adds positioning/naming evidence, not a replacement feature/price matrix (that remains MC-03A).

### 1.4 Limitations that bound every conclusion
1. **No installer, dealer or customer interviews.** Audience-split findings are read from vendor self-presentation, not from channel behaviour.
2. **No verification of ownership, revenue, market share or dates of availability.** Nothing here supports a "market leader" or "largest" statement about anyone.
3. **Warranty terms are region- and product-conditional.** Quoted terms are the reviewed English pages only; local Spanish/Portuguese terms may differ.
4. **JS-heavy sites hide content.** Absence of a promise on a reviewed page is not absence from the brand's marketing.
5. **Prices drift and differ by territory/VAT.** Two prices are quoted only because they illustrate tiering/naming, not to benchmark MAREA pricing (`MARKET-BENCHMARKS.md` remains the price-context doc).
6. **Certification:** the only certification-adjacent evidence found is an NMEA certified-products archive snippet listing a Digital Yacht gateway. Garmin/Raymarine/B&G/Simrad product pages reviewed state NMEA 2000 network support, not a certification claim; this pack does not assert certification for any brand.
7. **Ownership:** corporate-parent relationships beyond what pages state (Raymarine UK Limited; Navionics "A Garmin Brand") were not verified and are not asserted.

## 7. Implications for MC-03 positioning

### 7.1 What the strategy can credibly claim (after its own evidence gates close)
1. **Refit-first framing is defensible and under-served.** An app-first entrant publicly validates the "dated, disconnected, slow" pain, while incumbents sell new flagship ecosystems and leave legacy bridging to specialist modules. MAREA can claim *upgrade scope*, not superiority.
2. **Installer accountability is the right trust model** — it is exactly how Raymarine (certified dealer + On-Board Warranty) and Garmin (authorised service) earn trust. MAREA can adopt the model; it cannot inherit the network, so the claim is a process promise, not a coverage claim.
3. **Honest compatibility/readiness disclosure can be a real differentiator** because no reviewed competitor does it publicly; it is cheap for MAREA to do and expensive for incumbents to imitate at brand level.
4. **Local/onboard operation and data provenance are legitimate evaluation criteria** used by the category (offline plans, hydrographic-office sourcing), so a transparent offline/data-rights stance is credible *once licensed*.
5. **Category naming conventions are learnable:** abstract family + descriptive function + size/edition. MAREA's distinctiveness must live in MAREA, not in the family suffixes.
6. **Standards membership (NMEA) and published compatibility lists are the credible technical trust devices** for a new entrant — cheaper and more honest than awards or superlatives.

### 7.2 What it must avoid claiming (observed claim-risk in competitor messaging that MAREA must not imitate)
- **Unverifiable superlatives/rankings:** "world's most innovative and recognised", "world's No. 1 choice", "industry's largest" — all self-claims on reviewed pages. Do not copy this register.
- **Certification-style language:** "NMEA 2000 Certified" only after NMEA approval; membership ≠ certification; the only certification-adjacent evidence found was an NMEA archive snippet for another company's gateway.
- **Chart/coverage breadth promises:** incumbents lock charts ("Navionics is not supported" on B&G/Simrad; BlueChart g3 incompatible with Navionics+ units). MAREA must state licensed regions and formats explicitly, never "all charts".
- **Warranty/support promises before terms exist:** competitors set a 2-year floor and sell extensions; publishing a weaker or undefined term is a downgrade, not a saving.
- **"Works with everything"/"integrates with all instruments":** category pages show enumerated compatibility, not universal claims.
- **"Turnkey" pilot/complete-helm pricing:** unsafe and unevidenced for a not-commercially-available brand.
- **"Cheaper", "more accurate", "safer", "AI-powered":** explicitly excluded by `MAREA-BRAND-STRATEGY.md` §7.2; no evidence exists.
- **Installer/dealer network claims** until agreements and training exist.

### 7.3 Where MAREA's credible wedge is (decisive)
- **Primary wedge:** *"modernise, don't replace"* — an installer-assessed refit scope (legacy instruments → HUB/NAV/AIS/SENSE in steps) with a **published multi-brand compatibility matrix**, an honest cannot-do list, and one accountable seller with local (Galicia / northern Portugal) service. The intersection *integrated refit scope + installer accountability + readiness honesty* is unoccupied in the reviewed material.
- **Secondary wedge:** **readiness/lifecycle transparency as product experience** — visible lifecycle state, exclusions and support policy at the point of decision. Position it as trust, not as a feature headline.
- **Tertiary wedge (conditional):** **offline/data-rights clarity** — explicit offline entitlement and data export, only after chart/data licensing is evidenced.
- **Do not lead with:** nine-family breadth (nine names are not nine products), price, generic "ecosystem", awards-style claims, or comparisons on accuracy/safety.
