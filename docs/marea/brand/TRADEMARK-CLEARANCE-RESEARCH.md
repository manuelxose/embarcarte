## 1. Scope and tested subject matter

The proposed brand is **MAREA** for the EMBARCARTE marine product family described in
`../COMMERCIAL-PORTFOLIO.md`:

- marine electronics hardware (HUB gateway, AIS receiver, SENSE sensors, DISPLAY unit, WATCH monitoring kit);
- navigation and weather software (NAV, WEATHER) delivered as apps and web services;
- cloud/SaaS fleet operations (FLEET);
- supervised vessel steering hardware (PILOT);
- professional installation, commissioning, maintenance and repair services;
- retail/e-commerce of the above and of compatible third-party equipment.

**Search matters requested and considered** (not all could be executed against a working register
interface — see §4): `MAREA`, `MAREA MARINE`, `MAREA NAV`, `MAREA HUB`, `MAREA AIS`, `MAREA PILOT`,
`MAREA SENSE`, `MAREA WATCH`, `MAREA DISPLAY`, `MAREA WEATHER`, `MAREA FLEET`; justified variants:
`M MAREA` (word + device form actually found in the registers), `LA MAREA`, `MAREA BARCELONA`,
`MAREA NAUTIC`, `MAREA SUB`, plural `MAREAS`, and the translation/equivalence `TIDE` / `MARÉE`.

**Suffix discipline.** Adding a generic or semi-generic suffix (`NAV`, `HUB`, `AIS`, `MARINE`, `FLEET`)
**does not avoid a conflict**. Where the earlier right contains the identical verbal element `MAREA`,
a composite such as `MAREA NAV` normally still contains that element and remains comparable; the
suffix also risks being found descriptive of the very goods. The registers below confirm this shape of
risk: the earlier rights that matter are `MAREA` / `M MAREA`, not `MAREA <suffix>`.

## 2. Formal research status

**MATERIAL CONFLICT INDICATED — SPECIALIST REVIEW REQUIRED**

Basis (all verified against official registry data, §5):

1. A **live European Union trade mark `M MAREA`, EUTM 018135609, Nice class 9, registered 2020-02-27,
   proprietor BRANDS 2020, S.L. (Barcelona)**, covers inter alia *"Mobile apps; Application software for
   mobile phones; Software and applications for mobile devices; Downloadable application software for

   registered 2016-10-29) and a **third live EUTM** (`M MAREA`, 018395967, classes 3/14/18/25) — i.e. it
   already occupies two of the four classes the MAREA programme would want (35 retail, 37 repair).
3. The dominant and only verbal element of all three marks is **MAREA**. The mark is figurative, but the
   Figurative/word element `MAREA` is identical to the proposed brand.
4. A published French opposition decision reports an opposition between an earlier figurative `M MAREA`
   and `MAREA CREATION`, with the tribunal holding that the shared term **MAREA** gives the signs strong
   visual and phonetic similarity (secondary source, §6 row 12). This is consistent with `MAREA` being
   treated as the distinctive, dominant element in opposition practice.
5. A commercial household brand **MAREA (marea.es)** has used the sign in Spain since 1995 and now sells
   *smartwatches with integrated GPS* and a companion iOS/Android app — direct commercial overlap with the
   "wearable + app + marine data" space, even though no registered right was verified for it beyond the
   BRANDS 2020 marks.

This status is **not** a finding of infringement, not a prediction of refusal, and not a conclusion that
MAREA cannot be used. It states that materially relevant earlier rights in overlapping classes and
territories have been **verified**, so the decision must go to qualified counsel before any publication,
filing or spend. See §14 for the counter-arguments that counsel must weigh.

### 2.1 Status calibration — why `MATERIAL CONFLICT INDICATED` and not `POTENTIAL CONFLICT`

status must be driven by the strongest *verified* evidence, not by the strongest *commercial* signal. The
commercial signals alone would support POTENTIAL. The verified register data supports more.

## 8. Distinctiveness analysis of `MAREA`

**Meaning.** `MAREA` is an ordinary word of the Spanish, Italian, Portuguese and Catalan lexicon meaning
**"tide"** (Spanish/Italian *marea*; Portuguese *maré*; Catalan *marea*). The WIPO record itself carries the
voluntary translation "*MAREA — tide*", which is useful evidence of how the office and the applicant
themselves characterised the word.

**Linguistic context for the relevant public.**

- **Spain (primary market, Galicia/Rías Baixas first):** every relevant consumer knows *marea* = tide. The
  word is also embedded in everyday Spanish idioms (e.g. *"marea"* as a crowd; *"contra viento y marea"*).
- **Portugal:** the cognate *maré* is the ordinary word; `MAREA` reads as transparently Spanish/Italian but
  is instantly understood in a marine context.
- **Italy:** *marea* is the ordinary word for tide; it is also an existing commercial name in diving
  apparel (§6.8).
- **EU English-speaking public:** `MAREA` has no English meaning, so for that part of the public it is an
  arbitrary/coined-looking word — **more distinctive**, and therefore **broader** in scope.

**Descriptiveness / suggestiveness assessment (research view, not a legal conclusion).**

- For **marine goods and services**, `MAREA` is at minimum **suggestive** and arguably **descriptive** of a
  characteristic of the goods: tide is a core parameter of navigation, weather, current and depth data —
  exactly the NAV/WEATHER feature set ("tides/currents/depth contexts"). A mark that names a physical
  phenomenon central to the product's function gives weak inherent protection in those classes.
- Against that, EUIPO accepted and registered a **plain word mark `MAREA`** for class 33 (EUTM 009532771),
  and the `M MAREA` device marks were accepted in classes 9/35/37. In class 9 the goods are not "tide
  measurement" as such, so *marea* is not directly descriptive of a smartwatch or a chartplotter app — it is
  allusive. Expect the office and courts to treat it as **weak-to-moderate** distinctiveness in class 9,
  and weaker still in classes 37/42 where "tide" says nothing specific.
- **Consequence for scope of protection:** a weak/suggestive mark enjoys a **narrower** scope — small
  differences may suffice to avoid confusion — *but* the corollary is also true: because `MAREA` is weak and
  widely used by third parties (tide apps, watches, wetsuits, wine, vehicles, a Miami marine retailer), the
  registry and courts will be slow to let any one trader monopolise it, and **coexistence disputes are
  likely**. For EMBARCARTE this cuts both ways: it may be *registrable* with a sufficiently distinctive
  device/composite, and hard to *enforce*.
- **Acquired distinctiveness:** none exists for MAREA in the programme's field — the brand has never been
  used in trade. No enhanced distinctiveness can be claimed, and it may not be claimed in any filing.

**Practical conclusion:** a pure word mark `MAREA` in class 9 for marine navigation software is the weakest
and most exposed option; a distinctive composite (device + word, or word + genuinely distinctive element)
is the only realistic filing shape, and even then the earlier `M MAREA` class-9 right must be addressed.

## 9. Likelihood-of-confusion reasoning (structured, not adjudicated)

Assessed against the verified `M MAREA` rights and the commercial signals. This section frames the analysis
counsel must perform; it does not conclude on confusion.

**(a) Similarity of signs.** The earlier marks' only verbal element is `MAREA`. The proposed sign is
`MAREA`. For the word-only comparison the signs are **identical**; against the figurative `M MAREA` the
verbal element is identical and the device element (an oval "M") is decorative. The French decision in §6.6
supports treating `MAREA` as dominant. **High similarity.**

**(b) Similarity of goods/services.**

- **Class 9:** the earlier list literally includes *"Mobile apps"*, *"Application software for mobile
  phones"*, *"Software and applications for mobile devices"*, *"Downloadable application software for
  smartphones and tablets"*, *"Computer application software for mobile phones and tablets"*. MAREA NAV is a
  navigation application for phone/tablet/desktop. On the wording, the goods **overlap directly** — a
  navigation app is a mobile app. The commercial contexts differ (wristwear data vs marine navigation) and
  the public differs in part (fashion-watch buyers vs boat owners), which is the main counter-argument.
  **Medium-to-high similarity on the register wording; contested in substance.**
- **Class 35:** earlier services are retail of watches/jewellery/clothing; proposed is retail of marine
  electronics. Same class, different goods retailed — normally **low-to-medium** similarity.
- **Class 37:** earlier is watch repair; proposed is marine electronics installation/repair. Same class,
  different objects — **low similarity**, but the *service nature* (repair of technical equipment) is
  analogous.
- **Class 42:** no verified earlier EU right; similarity assessment deferred.

**(c) Relevant public and channels.** Boat owners, installers and charter operators on one side; fashion
watch/smartwatch consumers on the other. Overlap exists at the "consumer electronics sold online" end
(the marea.es smartwatch is sold through the same e-commerce channel and app stores where MAREA NAV would
live). App-store adjacency is real: a consumer searching "Marea" in an app store will already meet the tide
app (§13) and the marea.es smartwatch companion app.

**(d) Coexistence and weakness.** Because `MAREA` is a common word already used by many unrelated traders,
there is a genuine argument for **peaceful coexistence** and for a narrow scope. That argument is weaker
where the earlier right is **identical in the verbal element and covers the same class-9 software wording**.

**(e) Honest bottom line.** On the register wording, class 9 is the problem; classes 35/37 are same-class
but different-sub-sector problems; class 42 is currently clean at EU level in the records read. The
overall picture is a real, non-trivial conflict risk that cannot be resolved by this agent and must not be
resolved by assertion.

## 11. The conflict triad

The risk does not come from one place; it comes from three directions at once.

**A. Commercial use of `Marea Marine` (`https://mareamarine.com/`) — a commercial conflict signal, not a
registered-right finding.** A Miami-based marine business trading as **Marea Marine** sells marine parts and
marine navigation electronics (its own catalogue page advertises "Marine Navigation Instruments &
Electronics … multifunction displays (MFDs), chartplotters, radar arrays, transducers, and autopilot
systems from leading brands like Raymarine and Garmin, alongside Zipwake and Bennett trim control
systems") and provides technical services, installation and boat management. Observed facts only: it is a
live commercial presence in **exactly** the trade EMBARCARTE targets, trading under `MAREA` in the marine
field. **No earlier registered right of Marea Marine was found or verified.** Its effect is on
**confusion risk and market entry**: it occupies the "Marea + marine" search space, it is a potential
objector or a potential prior user in the US, and it makes the brand harder to own in search and social
channels. Its mere existence is **not** an infringement finding and does not by itself block Spain/Portugal/EU.

**B. `MAREA` marks in the target classes.** Verified: `M MAREA` in **class 9** (EUTM 018135609), in
**classes 35 and 37** (EUTM 015190655) and in classes 3/14/18/25 (EUTM 018395967), all live and owned by
**BRANDS 2020, S.L.**, which additionally trades as the Spanish watch/jewellery house **Marea** (marea.es).
Unverified leads add a possible Spanish `MAREA` in class 35 and a possible French `MAREA` in class 9 for
nautical instruments. This is the **decisive** arm of the triad.

**C. Genericness of the tidal word.** `MAREA` is the ordinary word for "tide" in Spanish/Italian and is
already in commercial use across tide apps (`Marea` tide-forecast app on the App Store; `Marea Venezia`;
`Tu Marea`), diving apparel, wine, vehicles and a marine retailer. The word is **crowded and weak**. That
lowers the scope of protection the programme could obtain and makes enforceability doubtful even if
registration succeeds.

## 12. Open questions on the scope of protection (research view)

- Would a marine-navigation app and a smartwatch be held **similar** in class 9? The register wording says
  yes on its face ("mobile apps", "software for mobile devices"); commercial reality says the users differ.
  This is the single question that decides the class-9 conflict.
- Would a **figurative** earlier mark with an oval "M" be found similar enough to a plain word `MAREA`? The
  French decision in §6.6 suggests the verbal element carries the comparison.
- Does the earlier proprietor's **class 35/37** coverage of *watches* extend to *marine electronics retail /
  repair* in the eyes of the office or a court? Probably weak, but it is not zero.
- Can MAREA be filed with a **distinctive device/composite** that keeps the word but avoids the figurative
  collision? That is a design-and-counsel question, not a research question.
