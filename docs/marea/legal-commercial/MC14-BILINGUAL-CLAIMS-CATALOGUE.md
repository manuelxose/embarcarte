## 1. Purpose

The catalogue pairs each governed claim in Spanish and English and records its
class, publication status, channel, audience, evidence requirement, approver and
release gate. It distinguishes four states that marketing tends to blur:

- **Proposed product concept** — concept only; not available.
- **Defined intended functionality** — recorded in the product contracts;
  still not tested.
- **Tested functionality** — requires a test report or sea trial; currently none.
- **Licensed functionality** — requires a signed licence; currently none.
- **Commercially available product** — requires OPERATIONS READY, approved
  price, stock and all legal gates; currently none.

No claim is approved for public publication. The four internal Class A examples
demonstrate compliant wording; every public-status record is blocked. ES/EN
pairs must carry the same class, status, numbers and negation; a translation
that softens or strengthens a claim is treated as a different, prohibited claim.

## 5. Approval and re-review

A public claim requires: an evidence artefact on file, an approver sign-off, an
open release gate, and a bilingual parity check. Any change in lifecycle state,
licence, certification, price or support period triggers re-review. A published
claim found unsupported must be retracted within 24 hours and recorded.

## 6. Honest limitation

The catalogue is a governance instrument, not legal advice. Passing the
validator proves consistency of classes/statuses and parity of the recorded
pairs; it does not prove that a claim is lawful in every context, which remains
a human legal-review question.
